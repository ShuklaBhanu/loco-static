Established in 1876 under the Rajputana-Malwa Railway, The Loco Workshop Ajmer stands as one of the oldest and most distinguished railway workshops in Asia. For 150 years, it has been the beating heart of Indian Railways — manufacturing, overhauling and modernising the locomotives that connect a billion lives.

From steam to diesel, from the colonial era to an independent republic, through partition and progress — every chapter of India's modern story has been powered, in part, by the forges of Ajmer.

1876
Rajputana-Malwa Railway
Workshop established to service the growing rail network across Rajputana under the British Raj.
1944
Western Railway Integration
Consolidated under the Western Railway zone — expanded capacity and overhauled thousands of steam engines.
1990s
Diesel Modernisation
Transitioned from steam to diesel locomotive overhaul, becoming a centre of excellence for diesel traction.
2026
Sesquicentennial — 150 Years
A landmark anniversary celebrating a century and a half of engineering excellence, innovation and national service.

A Century &
Half of Milestones

🚂
1876
Founded under Rajputana-Malwa Railway
The first workshops were established in Ajmer as the rail network expanded across the Rajputana region under British administration.
⚒️
1920s
Steam Age Expansion
Peak steam era — the workshop overhauled over 200 steam locomotives annually, with a workforce of over 8,000 artisans and engineers.
🇮🇳
1947
Independent India's Workshop
Post-independence, the workshop became a symbol of self-reliance — continuing operations under the new Indian Railways administration.

Sesquicentennial Celebrations
150 years of unbroken service — marked with national recognition, a heritage museum, and the commissioning of a next-gen overhaul facility.
